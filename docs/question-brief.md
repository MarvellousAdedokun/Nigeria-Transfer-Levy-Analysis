# Question Brief: The ₦50 You Never See (PROVISIONAL)

Status: provisional until the source audit is finished.

## Working title
The ₦50 You Never See: what official reports say about Nigeria's electronic money transfer levy (EMTL)

## Context
Nigerians see a small levy deducted on many electronic transfers. The government publishes monthly reports on how money is shared between the federal, state and local governments (FAAC reports). EMTL appears as a line in those reports. I want to know what these official reports can and cannot tell us about the levy.

## Primary question
What do official Nigerian reports tell us about EMTL amounts over time, and how is the money shared if the reports show that detail?

## Audience
A fintech strategy or analytics team, a payments researcher, a journalist, or a public-finance analyst. These are possible readers, not confirmed sponsors.

## Hypotheses and the evidence that would prove or disprove each
| Hypothesis | Supported if | Disproved if |
|---|---|---|
| H1: Reported EMTL amounts vary a lot between periods | Clear differences after checking periods and units | Amounts are flat, or differences come only from period or unit mix-ups |
| H2: Some recipients or states get more than others | A consistent state-level breakdown exists and shows differences | No consistent breakdown across reports |
| H3: A documented policy or reporting change coincides with a change in the figures | A reliable source gives the change and its date, and figures shift around it | No reliable source, or no shift |

H3 shows timing only. It does not prove cause.

## Definitions still unresolved
- Is each figure the revenue month, the month it was shared, or the publication month? (I must not rely on the filename.)
- Does each figure measure money collected or money shared?
- What exactly is included in the "Others" grouping?
- Which unit is each table in?
- The dates of any policy changes (for example when fintech apps began deducting the levy, and what changed in 2026) need a reliable source. Press articles disagree on the 2026 rule.

## Known constraints
- Public sources only.
- The reports show allocations. They probably do not show transfer counts, per-person amounts, or amounts per fintech.
- Some months may be missing or formatted differently.

## What this project will NOT claim
- How many transfers Nigerians make.
- How much any single fintech collects.
- That a policy change caused a change in the figures.
